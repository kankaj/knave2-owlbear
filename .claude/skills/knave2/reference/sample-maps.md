## Sample hex map (pp. 78–79, Kyle Latino)

A two-page spread of a **12 × 7 hex grid** in the six-mile-hex convention (Traveling, p. 8). Columns are numbered **1–12** along the top, rows **1–7** down the left. Hexes are pointy-topped in **offset columns** — odd columns sit half a hex higher than even ones, which is why the column labels alternate height. A **d12** icon at the top right and a **d8** icon at the bottom left mark the axes: roll d12 for column and d8 for row to pick a random hex (row 8 is off-map, so an 8 needs a reroll or a "nothing" result — the book doesn't say).

Terrain (west to east): sea along the west and south coasts (shaded), a bay in the north with a river running inland toward the east, hills and a wooded headland, a swamp and marsh belt in the middle, a **volcano** with a lava field, a **geyser or hot spring**, a mountain range in the south-east, and a **mushroom forest** in the far south-east corner. Non-named features include a shipwreck (column 7, row 1), farm fields and a ruined tower (columns 1–2, rows 1–2), a village of huts (column 2, row 5), standing stones, ruins, and a giant spiderweb.

Named locations, read from the labels:

Every name is a structure or location (p. 10–11) plus a place trait or theme — the map demonstrates the tables in use. It also demonstrates the travel rules: three hexes a day means crossing the map takes about four days of travel.

## Sample dungeon map (pp. 76–77, Kyle Latino)

An **isometric cutaway** of a small multi-level dungeon, drawn as a keyed-less "living" picture rather than a floor plan. What it shows, top to bottom:

- **Surface**: a graveyard with headstones and a **chapel** (pews, lectern, arched windows, a door) — the entrance. A skeleton with a key sits in the grass beside an open grave.
- **Level 1**: a **shrine room** with an altar, candles, and crossed effigies; a **secret door** marked **S**; a hall with a carved sun-face idol and rubble; a **portcullis** with a chain mechanism; stairs down.
- **Level 2**: a **treasury** (chests, spilled coins, a brazier), reached through another **S** secret door; a **cell block** with a barred door and a tentacled thing behind it, a prisoner, and pigs; a **pit** in the floor; a lever pillar.
- **Caverns** (right page): a chained **minotaur-like brute**; a **floating island** with a well-house; a pool from which stone hands rise, with a dropped bucket; a cave with a dragon skull and bones; a **skull-faced tombstone** shrine; and an alchemist's **forge room** with a furnace and a coffin-like trough.

The map illustrates the Delving chapter: secret doors found by searching, a portcullis as a **mechanism** (p. 17), rooms drawn from the **rooms** table (chapel, crypt, cell, treasury, forge, shrine, cavern), and hazards (pit, tentacled thing, chained monster). Nothing on it carries rules text; the "S" markers are the only notation.
