# Spells and magic tables (pp. 21–31)

## Spellcasting rules (p. 21)

- A **spellbook** takes one item slot and contains **one spell**. PCs cannot create or copy spellbooks; they are found in dungeons or stolen from other magic-users.
- **Casting** is one action. Each book works **once per day**. A PC may use up to **INT** spellbooks per day.
- **INT in a spell text** = any number the caster chooses **up to their INT**. That number is the spell's **level** where one matters (saves, *spellseize*, *unravel*).
- **Item** = liftable with one hand. **Object** = anything up to human size.
- Unless stated otherwise, ongoing effects last **10 minutes (1 turn)** and range is **40′**.
- **Saves**: when a spell targets an **unwilling** creature whose **level is higher than the spell's level**, the target may make a check vs the spell's level (target `11 + spell level`; a monster uses its level as its ability). Success **halves** the effect; success by **10 or more nullifies** it. Lower-level and willing targets have no save.
- **Chaos spellbooks** replace their spell with a random new one (rolled from the list below, or generated) at the first dawn after the previous spell was cast.
- **Damage**: none of the 100 spells deals direct damage by design. A generated damaging spell should deal about **INT × d6**, less if it also has other benefits.
- **Classic spellbooks** from other old-school games (p. 73): treat each leveled spell as a whole book castable only by a PC with **INT ≥ the spell's level**; once per day; counts toward the INT limit. **Scrolls** from such modules can be cast by anyone regardless of INT, do not count toward the daily limit, and cannot be transcribed into books.
- A **lich** owns 10 randomly generated spellbooks; an **eye tyrant** fires a random spell from each of 10 eye stalks per round — the list below is the source for both.

## Generating new spells (p. 27)

For new spellbooks or chaos books: roll a **formula** on d12, replace each bracketed word with a result from the matching table, adjust the wording, and agree the effect with the players. Example from the book: formula 10 → "[Name]'s [Quality] [Element] [Form]" → "Asterwin's Iridescent Tears Circle" → *Asterwin's Iridescent Circle of Tears*: a shimmering circle INT × 10′ wide; creatures inside pass a WIS check each round or take −5 to all checks that round; creatures that cannot cry are unaffected.
