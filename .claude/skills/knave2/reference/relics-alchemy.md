# Relic magic and alchemy (pp. 32–37)

## Relic magic (p. 32)

Divine magic in Knave is **relational**: a cleric-type PC carries relics the way a wizard carries spellbooks, but power flows from staying in a patron's favor.

- **Patrons** are powerful, often incorporeal magical beings — petty gods, saints, nature spirits, outsiders. Treat them as NPCs with **goals** (p. 57), a **personality** (p. 56), and **mannerisms** (p. 59). Each cares about **one or two domains** (below), positively or negatively.
- **Relics** are magic items bound to a patron: a symbol, weapon, garment, or anything else (item tables pp. 39–43 for inspiration). Usually found in dungeons or at shrines; a patron may also convert a PC's own item. A relic always takes **at least one slot**.
- **Shrines** are where PCs talk to a patron — only with the patron's favor **and** one of its relics in hand. Cities have shrines to every patron; towns d6 shrines; villages usually one.
- **Blessings.** Find a relic → speak to the patron at a shrine → receive a **mission** (p. 51) → complete it and return → the patron imbues the relic with an **ongoing blessing** tied to its domain: something small but useful, e.g. an aura or a minor spell usable several times a day, designed **with** the player (magic tables pp. 27–31, potions p. 35, powers p. 65 help). It lasts as long as the PC keeps the relic and the favor.
- **Limits.** Any number of relics; **active blessings ≤ CHA**, chosen each morning.
- **Favor and disfavor.** Acting against a patron's goals may cost the blessing; regaining favor means another mission or an atonement. Devoted service can earn stronger blessings, more relics, or followers (p. 53). Serving several patrons with incompatible goals is a built-in source of dilemmas.

## Alchemy (p. 35)

- **Potion effects** produce one significant magical effect. Ongoing effects (invisibility) last **one turn (10 min)**; weak ones (beast-speech) an hour or even a day. A **drop** tasted gives a clue to the effect.
- **Brewing** needs a **fire, a cauldron, ingredients, and one watch (4 hours)**. The player states the effect, duration, and ingredients (usually monster parts, but any rare substance with the right properties). If the GM approves, the PC makes an **INT check** (+5 for spending a second watch). Success: potion made. **Ingredients are consumed either way.** Success by **10+** yields a **recipe**: with those exact ingredients no further check is needed.
- **Harvesting** a monster part takes **one turn (10 min)** and proper tools; harvested parts and magical plants take **at least one slot** because of packaging and preservative fluids. Parts carry the ability they granted the monster — a dragon's lung for fire breath, its scales for fireproofing.
