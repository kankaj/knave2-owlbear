# Exploration: traveling, delving, encounters (pp. 8–19)

Both exploration modes share one engine (adapted from Brendan S.'s Hazard System, CC BY 3.0): time advances in fixed units, and at the end of each unit a **hazard die** (d6) is rolled and applied.

## Traveling (p. 8)

- **Watches.** The day has **six 4-hour watches**: three day, three night. Most major actions (travel, forage, search, explore, rest) take one watch.
- **Travel speed.** One **6-mile hex per watch**, up to **three watches per day** for free. Every further travel watch that day deals **1 direct damage** to each PC unless they pass a **CON check**. Speed is **halved** in darkness, difficult terrain, or severe weather, and **doubled** when riding.
- **Navigation.** In disorienting terrain or weather the GM may secretly roll a **WIS check for the party leader**; failure moves the party to a random adjacent hex.
- **Exploring.** Spend a watch in a hex to reveal areas of interest that passing through would miss (overgrown ruin, hidden pool).
- **Secret features** (buried treasure, a hidden door into a mountain) have **clues** revealed by exploring, and are found only by interacting with the world — never by a roll.
- **Foraging.** One watch and a **WIS check** (modified by weather and terrain) → **d6 rations**.
- **Water.** Assume PCs find drinking water while traveling unless the environment is arid.

### Travel hazard die (end of every watch)

| d6 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Encounter** — roll on a regional/terrain table; GM sets reaction, activity, distance, surprise (see Encounters) |
| 2 | **Fatigue** — each PC takes 1 damage unless they spend the next watch resting (more in severe weather/terrain); ignore while resting |
| 3 | **Depletion** — roll d6 per perishable item (rations, monster parts…); on a 1 it has spoiled |
| 4 | **Travel shift** — the weather changes (reroll weather) or a local event begins (travel shifts table) |
| 5 | **Sign** — a sign that a random encounter is nearby; the next encounter rolled in this hex is that creature. Or reveal a clue to something hidden |
| 6 | **Free** — nothing |

## Weather (p. 9)

Roll at the start of the game and whenever the travel hazard die shows 4.

| 2d6 | Weather |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Thunderstorm; blizzard in winter |
| 3 | Heavy rain; heavy snow in winter |
| 4 | Cold for the season |
| 5 | Light rain; snow in winter |
| 6 | Cool for the season |
| 7 | Mild for the season |
| 8 | Warm for the season |
| 9 | Windy |
| 10 | Hot for the season |
| 11 | Strong winds |
| 12 | Hail in spring, humidity in summer, fog in fall, frost in winter |

Effects: **rain** soaks everything; heavy rain cuts visibility, disorients, slows, drowns sound, makes mud, eventually floods. **Hail** cuts visibility, disorients, slows, drowns sound. **Lightning** spooks animals and ignites things; strikes PCs (3d6) only if they invite it. **Wind** carries scents and hinders ranged attacks; with rain or dust it cuts visibility; strong wind slows, drowns sound, knocks things over. **Fog** cuts visibility and disorients. **Snow** cuts visibility and slows; heavy snow disorients. **Temperature** extremes raise fatigue damage for the unprepared; freezing or scorching weather may damage every watch.

## Delving (p. 13)

- **Turns.** Dungeon time is tracked in **10-minute turns**. Moving, searching, fighting, resting each take a turn.
- **Speeds per turn:**
  - **Crawling, 120′.** Mostly standing still, listening, testing surfaces, mapping. PCs **automatically detect traps** and map.
  - **Walking, 2,400′.** Surprised by all encounters, spring all traps, can still map. For backtracking through cleared areas.
  - **Running, 4,800′.** Surprised, spring traps, **cannot map**, **candles blow out**. Last resort.
- **Light:**
  - **Candles** — shapes within 20′, detail within 5′; last **8 hours** (a whole expedition); **10 per slot**. Cheap insurance against total darkness.
  - **Lanterns** — a candle in a glass-and-metal case, optionally shuttered; same duration; **cannot be blown out**; takes a whole slot.
  - **Torches** — shapes within 40′, detail within 10′; a whole slot each; **burn out when the hazard die shows 3**; a new torch lights from the embers of the old.
  - **Total darkness** — **−10** on checks involving movement or coordination; surprised by all encounters; spring all traps; cannot map.
- **Searching.** One turn per room reveals all non-obvious features (a statuette in a drawer, a cracked tile) and all clues to secrets. In **dim light** (candle, lantern) it takes **two turns**. Obvious features are described immediately; details emerge as players investigate.

### Dungeon hazard die (end of every turn)

| d6 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Encounter** — roll on the dungeon's table; GM sets reaction, activity, distance, surprise |
| 2 | **Fatigue** — each PC takes 1 damage unless they spend the next turn resting (more in harsh dungeons); ignore while resting |
| 3 | **Burn** — lit torches burn out; relight from embers |
| 4 | **Delve shift** — the environment changes; if the dungeon has no obvious shifts use sounds, temperature, wind, debris, apparitions, vermin (delve shifts table) |
| 5 | **Sign** — a sign of a nearby encounter (that creature is met at the next encounter in this dungeon), or a clue to something hidden |
| 6 | **Free** — nothing |

## Encounters (p. 19)

Procedure for any random encounter.

1. **Distance.** Confined spaces with limited sight (dungeons): **2d6 × 10′**. Wilderness, large caverns, open ground: **4d6 × 30′**.
2. **Surprise.** If within **80′**, the closest PC makes a **WIS check vs the WIS of the closest encountered creature** (a monster uses its level, half, or 0 — unobservant undead are 0). Whoever wins by **5 or more** has surprised the other side: they **act first** and get **+5 on all combat checks in the first round**. Note the dungeon speeds above: walking or running means being surprised automatically.
3. **Activity.** What the creatures are doing — they should have a life beyond waiting for the PCs. Pick or roll below, ignoring nonsense.
4. **Reaction.** If not obvious, roll 2d6. PC behaviour can change it fast.

| 2d6 | NPC reaction |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Kill the PCs |
| 3 | Injure or capture the PCs |
| 4 | Harass or rob the PCs |
| 5 | Insult, threaten, or command the PCs |
| 6 | Avoid the PCs |
| 7 | Ignore the PCs |
| 8 | Follow or observe the PCs |
| 9 | Greet or question the PCs |
| 10 | Share information with the PCs |
| 11 | Perform minor favors for the PCs |
| 12 | Ask to join the PCs' party |
